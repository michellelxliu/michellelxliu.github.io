Universal basic income in a society of LLM agents

A replication of Section 7.4 of AgentSociety (Piao et al., 2025, arXiv:2502.08691): two simulated economies of 100 Texas residents, each run for 120 months by Claude Opus 5.5 agents. In one, every citizen receives an unconditional $1,000 a month from month 96. The paper reports that the payment raised consumption and lowered depression (CES-D). This page shows what the replication found, and lets you step through every month and read each citizen's decision.

Method

What was replicated

The paper's text gives §7.4 only in outline. The protocol is the engine's own v1 code (the agentsociety package: MonthEconomyPlanBlock, the firm, bank, government and statistics-bureau agents, and examples/UBI/main.py). Each month, every citizen gets one fixed prompt (below) and answers two numbers: a propensity to work and the share of wealth to spend on goods. Income is hours × hourly wage; the spending budget buys goods from one firm at the going price; the government taxes income with the 2020 US federal brackets; the bank credits interest and sets its rate by a Taylor rule; the firm moves its price and wages with excess demand. Every third month the citizen fills in the 20-item CES-D; in the UBI arm, at months 96 and 108, they give an opinion. The payment starts at month 96, as in the paper's Figure 20, and the two arms are compared over months 96–119.

Who the citizens are

100 people from NVIDIA's Nemotron-Personas-USA (CC BY 4.0): Texas residents aged 18–64 with an occupation, drawn in proportion to the state's sex × age-decade distribution, with the record's one-sentence persona. Hourly wages are the BLS OEWS May 2023 Texas median hourly wage of each person's occupation (mean $30.16, median $26.29; the Texas all-occupation median is $22.10). Everyone starts with $30,000 in savings, as in the original code.

What the model received

Both arms run Claude Opus 5.5 at low reasoning effort. This page's runs went through claude -p on a subscription login, from an empty directory with no tools, no settings and a one-line neutral system prompt; the host adds one line naming the Claude Agent SDK, a reminder with the account's email, and an environment block (platform, model, date). Where this departs from the original code, and why


    

  1. Only the monthly economic loop is simulated. The original citizens also lived a daily life (movement, social contact, needs); none of it enters the §7.4 measures, and it would have multiplied the model calls about thirty-fold. The daily shopping action is replaced by one deterministic purchase per month: floor(budget / price) units, as much as the firm has in stock.
    2. 

  2. A floor on the policy rate at −0.5% a year. The released Taylor rule has none; when prices fell about 17% in a year it set −6.4%. Real central banks have gone no lower than about −0.75%, so the rate is floored at −0.5% (the ECB deposit rate's low, 2019–22).
    3. 

  3. The UBI arm starts from the control arm's exact state at month 96. The paper ran two separate simulations. Here both arms share months 0–95, so any gap after month 96 is the payment plus sampling noise, with no drift from the early months mixed in.
    4. 

  4. Bank units corrected. The released code computes yearly inflation in percent but compares it with 0.02, and credits the whole rate every month — 10% inflation would give a 502% monthly rate. Here inflation is a fraction and the rate is annual, credited as rate/12 (the released rule is available as --rules v1).
    5. 

  5. Taxes are actually collected. The released government agent waited on a field no code ever set, so it never reached its tax routine and every prompt said "Your tax deduction amounted to $0.00". Here the bracket table the paper and the prompt describe is applied to monthly income (--tax none reproduces the released behaviour). The prompt's sentence about redistributing the revenue is kept verbatim; no redistribution happens, as in the released code.
    6. 

  6. CES-D scored as published (items 4, 8, 12, 16 reversed). The released code's reversal never fired on JSON answers; its literal score is also stored.
    7. 

  7. Real GDP is the month's units sold at month-0 prices; the original computed it inside a closed binary.
    8. 

  8. Persona sentence added after the first paragraph; the original prompt named only name, age, city and job.
    9. 

  9. Replayed exchange is text only. The model's earlier reasoning is not sent back (the original stored text as well).
    10. 

  10. Hourly wages from BLS by occupation instead of the original's random Pareto draw (ten levels from about $12 to $420 an hour).
    11. 



Limits


    

  • The claude -p host returns no reasoning text and no token log-probabilities; both facts are recorded on every call. At low effort the model rarely thinks here (pilot: median 0 thinking tokens, maximum 52).
    • 

  • One model, one seed, two arms: no confidence intervals. Differences between arms are the model's response to the payment plus the economy's randomness (the firm's price and wage moves, the order in which citizens shop).
    • 

  • The model may know the paper and the policy debate; nothing in the prompt names the study, but the design of the prompt is public.
    • 



The real-world comparison the paper makes


Bartik et al. (2024, NBER w32784; the OpenResearch unconditional cash study, $1,000 a month for three years to 1,000 low-income adults in Texas and Illinois, against $50 a month for 2,000 controls) found spending up by at least $300 a month, mostly on housing, food and cars, with no lasting change in net worth. The companion health paper (Miller et al., 2024, NBER w32711) found that the early gains in stress and mental health did not persist beyond the first year. The paper's "UBI lowers depression" matches the first-year result only.
