Goal

Gracia-Lázaro et al. (2012, PNAS; "GL12") had 1,229 Aragonese secondary students play a weak Prisoner's Dilemma on a 25×25 lattice or a heterogeneous network: 51 rounds with fixed neighbours, then 59 rounds with everyone moved to a random node each round. Ferraz de Arruda et al. (2026, arXiv 2606.30454; "FdA26") replaced them with Llama 4 agents. The agents matched the cooperation curve but not the spread of individual cooperation rates or the moody-conditional-cooperation (MCC) rule. This study diagnoses which design choices cause that macro–micro gap, with a full factorial scored against all three human signatures, and one arm that reproduces FdA26.

Round loop

Each round every player gets one stateless call: the system prompt holds the persona and the tutorial they have seen so far; the user message holds the memory block and the choice instruction. The reply is JSON with the choice first and a short reason (a post-hoc rationale). P(cooperate) is read from the top-5 logprobs at the choice token. Then 10% of moves are replaced by the computer's rule, payoffs are computed per link, and each player's history grows by the screen they saw. State is snapshotted every round, so a run resumes and Part II branches from round 51.

2 × 4 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 64 cells, each on both networks; the fixed phase runs once per 32 cells and Part II branches from its snapshot. Side cells: the exact FdA26 replication; rationale before choice; rich_coop (rich persona plus PISA 2003 cooperative/competitive-learning items). Fixed everywhere: model (llama4:16x17b first), temperature 0.8, choice first, counterbalanced colours (pairs GREEN/BROWN, BLUE/YELLOW, ORANGE/PURPLE, BLACK/WHITE, PINK/GREY), the timeout rule, probes on every agent.

Personas

Minimal: age, final-year student in Aragón, gender. Rich: one real PISA 2009 Aragón student per seat (1,514 records; pool of 988 modal-grade students), matched on gender, rendered by a fixed template. PISA 2009 has no cooperation, competition, teamwork or trust items, so those are tested separately in rich_coop with PISA 2003's ten maths-class learning items, matched by gender and ESCS percentile.

The example below is an invented record rendered by the real template, not a real student.

Recognition probes

After rounds 1, 51 and 110, on every agent, on copies of its prompt (a test checks state files are byte-identical before and after):

  • ic in character: "(Think it over for a moment: what do you suppose this experiment is for? Answer briefly, in your own words.)"
  • familiar (after Part II, the humans' own item): "Is something in the experiment familiar to you (yes/no)? If so, please point out of what it reminds you."
  • ooc out of character, the prompt shown as a passage: "Does this passage remind you of anything you know: a game, a historical event, a scientific study, or anything else? If it does, say what, and how confident you are. If it doesn't, say so." / "Suppose this scenario is based on a real, published study. Which study is it most likely to be? Give your best guess even if you are unsure, and your confidence from 0 to 100."
  • mem and know, once per model: continue the tutorial verbatim (with an invented control passage); what the model recalls of GL12.

Metrics (fixed before any run)

Per network × phase, against the human data: cooperation curve (RMSE, r, r on first differences); spread of individual rates (SD, Wasserstein-1, with FdA26's Bernoulli null); persistence; MCC a, b by weighted least squares as GL12, plus per-agent MCC from logprobs; network effect (expected none); recognition rates by time point and degree. Replaced (timeout) moves are excluded from agent scoring. The offline stand-in, playing MCC with GL12's lattice values, is recovered by the analysis (after C: 0.446 / 0.130 vs 0.457 / 0.122; after D: 0.352 / −0.153 vs 0.350 / −0.149).

Model and serving

  • llama4:16x17b is one 67.4 GB Q4_K_M GGUF (108.6B parameters), with the vision tower in the same file; llama.cpp is dropped, Ollama serves the exact weights. Ollama returns top-5 logprobs (since Nov 2025), tested with v0.18.2.
  • How FdA26's prompts reached Ollama depends on their CrewAI version: before 1.12.0 (26 Mar 2026) LiteLLM flattened the messages into one "### System / ### User" prompt under Ollama's default "You are Llama 4…" system prompt, at Ollama's defaults (temperature 0.8, top_k 40, top_p 0.9, repeat penalty 1.1); from 1.12.0, OpenAI-style chat with temperature defaulting to 1.0. Asked of the authors.
  • Plan: Ollama for the FdA26 arm and a reference baseline; SGLang for the factorial if the baseline cell agrees.

Choices to review

Made while building; none is tuned to data. Mark each and add notes; they are saved in this browser only.

C1ooc probe passage
The out-of-character probe shows the player's current prompt (persona, tutorial, current memory block) as the passage. Calls are stateless, so that prompt is everything the player has; there is no longer transcript.
C2Fresh Part II players
Fresh players see the same tutorial (pages 1–12) as carried-over ones; only memory and running totals differ (totals restart at 0).
C3'X' on tutorial page 11
The Part I number of neighbours. On the heterogeneous network the number changes every Part II round; what GL12's screen said is not published.
C4Timed-out moves
The model is still called on a move the timeout draw replaces. Its intended choice is logged; history and neighbours see the computer's move, as humans saw it. Scoring excludes replaced moves.
C5Unreadable replies
After 3 attempts the computer's move is used and the row is flagged unreadable. The FdA26 arm uses their rule: 10 tries, then GREEN.
C6Round-1 timeout
A fair coin (no previous action exists; GL12 does not say).
C7FdA26 arm details
No timeouts; their payoff description repeated every round; round numbers restart at 0 in Part II; backstory STUDENT_AND_GENDER plus a degree line; CrewAI wrapping approximated. All wait on their code and CrewAI version.
C8Memory (iv) wording thresholds
'Rising'/'falling' = the exponential average moved more than 0.03 in a round; 'about as usual' = within 0.5 ECU per link; tags: running surprise average (weight 0.4), 'mostly chooses X' at ≥ 0.7 or ≤ 0.3, else 'switches'. On the lattice every neighbour who ever switched is tagged.
C9Memory (v) consolidation prompt
The engine's MEMORY.md prompt with two edits so no prompt says 'simulated'. 8 recent episodes, 4,000-character MEMORY.md, ≤1,500 tokens per consolidation.
C10Age 17 for everyone
The on-time age of the cohort on 20 December 2011.
C11Provisional colour pairs
GREEN/BROWN, BLUE/YELLOW, ORANGE/PURPLE, BLACK/WHITE, PINK/GREY until the authors send GL12's five pairs.
C12Fallback heterogeneous network
Drawn from P(k) ∝ k^−2.7, k = 2–16, 604 nodes; mean degree 3.16 (GL12 does not publish its mean).
C13Persona pool
Only modal-grade PISA students (4º ESO at 15; 988 of 1,514 Aragón records), since students who had repeated rarely reached 2º Bachillerato on time. Records with no questionnaire answers are left out.
C14Persona content
Scores, age, grade and repetition are not rendered. Parents' jobs are ISCO-88 group titles in English.
C15rich_coop matching
The 2003 items come from a Spanish 2003 student of the same gender at the same within-gender ESCS percentile (2003 has no Aragón sample; items are about maths classes).
C16Serving
Ollama v0.18.2 (exact Q4_K_M weights) for the FdA26 arm and a reference baseline; SGLang for the factorial if the baseline cell agrees. llama.cpp dropped.
C17Temperature
0.8 everywhere for now. It equals FdA26's only if their CrewAI was older than 1.12.0; on 1.12+ Ollama's OpenAI route defaults to 1.0.
