Networked Prisoner's Dilemma: our prompt design vs. the published LLM run

ResultDifferencesRound 1 promptsLater roundsNextNotes

We are replicating the human experiment of Gracia-Lázaro et al. (2012, PNAS; "GL12": 1,229 Spanish secondary-school students playing a weak Prisoner's Dilemma on networks) with LLM agents, building on Ferraz de Arruda et al. (2026, arXiv 2606.30454; "FdA26"), who ran the same game with llama4. This page sets our prompt design beside theirs, as both were sent to the same model (Llama 4 Scout) in our pilot of 27 October 2026.

Result: the two designs produce different games

Share of players cooperating in each round of Part I on the lattice. Our design keeps llama4 cooperating almost fully; FdA26's design, run by us, falls towards their published level after round 5; humans sit in between and decline slowly.

No version of FdA26's setup reproduces their round 1 (0.70): ours open at 0.97–0.99, with round-1 replies arguing for the "mutually beneficial" choice almost word for word. The chat route with a summary after round 1 is the closest (RMSE 0.24) and matches their level from round 6.

What differs between the two designs

ElementOur designFdA26 (their templates, as CrewAI sends them)
Message structureA system message (the on-screen directions, then the persona) and one user message per round (the round screen).No system message. CrewAI, with both of their templates set, sends one user message: their system template, "Answer the task below in valid JSON format:", the task, and CrewAI's expected-output lines. Ollama then applies the model's default system prompt.
DirectionsGL12's directions in full, pages 1–9 before Part I (about 1,070 words): the experiment, two parts of 50–70 rounds, earnings paid in euros, the virtual network, fixed neighbours in Part I, payoffs, examples, timing.A subset starting at "Possible payoffs per neighbor" (about 490 words): payoff table, examples, timing. No framing of the experiment, the parts, the network or real money.
Who the neighbours are"People" / "participants" in GL12's wording (the pilot's cells did not add FdA26's framing)."your neighbors (which are humans)".
Who the model isA persona: a 17-year-old in the final year of secondary school in Aragón, in an experiment in the school's computer room (rich cells add PISA-based background).Nothing. Their role, backstory (high-school student, gender) and goal ("maximize your own ECUs") are dropped by CrewAI when both templates are set.
ColoursOne of five colour pairs per player; which colour cooperates, and the order shown, vary by player.Always GREEN (cooperate) and BROWN (defect), GREEN listed first.
Each round's feedbackMemory (iv): last round's own choice, earnings and running total; each neighbour's choice and scaled earnings; the round before; whether earnings were better or worse than usual; notable neighbours.Previous round only: each neighbour's choice and payoff, own choice and payoff. No totals, no longer history. In the repeat reading the full description is sent again every round.

Timeouts10% of moves are the computer's (as for GL12's humans), shown as "the system chose … for you".None.

Next: which element keeps llama4 cooperating?

A small diagnostic cell changes our design one element at a time towards FdA26's (the rows of the table above): directions subset, persona removed, "(which are humans)", fixed GREEN/BROWN, previous-round-only feedback, reply format, sampling. Each version runs a few rounds of the lattice; the element that moves round-1 cooperation from about 0.97 towards 0.6 is the one that matters.

Notes

  • Our pilot's system message carried the heading "About you:" twice (a template bug, since fixed); the prompt shown here is the corrected one.
  • FdA26's code and CrewAI version are unpublished; their arm is rebuilt from their SI S1.4.1 and CrewAI's source (1.11.0, the same in 0.150.0). The chat route is CrewAI 1.12+; the native route is CrewAI before 1.12 via LiteLLM.
  • Human curve: recovered from GL12 Fig. 2A; FdA26 curve: recovered from FdA26 Fig. 2a. Both are read from the vector figures; checks against published numbers pass.
