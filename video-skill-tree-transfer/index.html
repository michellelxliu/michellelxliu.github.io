Does a skill tree predict what fine-tuning transfers to? Video results so far

Qwen3.5-9B, LoRA fine-tuning on video-benchmark questions grouped by skill. Michelle Liu, updated 2026-11-11. Work in progress: one model; the second model and the unfrozen-vision rerun are not finished.

In short
  • Training on one skill group raises accuracy on held-out questions of that group (+5.0 pp in p(correct) beyond random training) and, more to the point, on unseen sibling skills more than on distant skills: sibling minus far = +4.21 pp (permutation p = 0.0005; 95 % CI [+2.96, +5.58]), positive in 14 of 16 held-out siblings.
  • Much of it appears without the video. Blind, the sibling effect is +2.58 pp (p = 0.003); the part that needs the video is +1.64 pp (p = 0.058).
  • One skill group carries most of it. Camera Motion pairs average +12.0 pp; the other 13 pairs average +1.7 pp with video (+1.1 blind).
  • The tree beats a semantic-embedding grouping on sibling transfer (+4.2 vs +2.6 pp with video; difference +1.6 [−0.0, +3.6]; blind +1.9 [+0.1, +3.5]), but the two groupings predict item-level gains about equally and each adds to the other.
  • Memorising answer wordings does not produce sibling transfer. A text-only learner that only memorises which option wordings were correct in training gets +2.1 pp of within-group specificity but -1.0 pp sibling-vs-far, against +4.2 measured.
  • Unfrozen vision tower (interim, 20 of 78 runs): against their frozen-vision twins, accuracy barely moves overall (+0.16 pp with video, +0.10 blind); blind gains have not fallen.

The question

Video benchmarks test many skills at once. We labelled each benchmark question with the skills it needs, organised into a tree: broad skills (L2, e.g. Camera Motion Perception) with finer skills under them (L3, e.g. Judging camera translation direction). If the tree describes real structure in what a model learns, then fine-tuning on questions of some skills should transfer more to their siblings in the tree than to distant skills, even when the sibling's questions were never trained on.

Design

Questions and labels
Multiple-choice questions from 22 public video benchmarks (e.g. TempCompass, TemporalBench, PhysBench, MotionBench, NExT-QA, MVBench), each labelled with L2/L3 skills by an LLM labeller. Questions are reworded (3 meaning-preserving rewordings per question, checked by a separate judge) so training does not rely on one exact wording. Videos up to 32 s, 2 fps.
Training arms (300 questions each, 3 seeds each)
Whole: all of one L2 (7 L2s used). Leave-one-out: the same L2 with one L3 held out entirely (16 such pairs). Random: 300 random questions (3 arms), the reference every gain is measured against. Same recipe for all: LoRA rank 16 on the language model, 1 epoch (~1,170 examples), loss on the answer letter only.
Test
2,129 held-out questions (never trained on by any arm), scored with video and blind (same prompt, no video). Score: the model's probability on the correct letter, p(correct). Gain = change from the base model, minus the average change from random training.
Measures (fixed before results)
C1 within-group: gain on the arm's own L2's test questions (specificity = that minus gain elsewhere). C2 sibling vs far, the main test: for each leave-one-out arm, gain on the held-out L3 minus mean gain on L3s of other L2s, averaged over the 16 pairs. C3 own vs sibling: whole-L2 gain minus leave-one-out gain on the held-out L3. p values from permutations of the tree; intervals from resampling test questions.

Base model: 74.4 % accuracy with video, 54.5 % blind, 37.8 % chance. The text alone already answers many questions; this matters for the blind results.

Benchmark and subtask groupings (post hoc)

Arms built from one benchmark or one benchmark subtask show within-group specificity of +4.1 and +5.1 pp with video (tree +6.1), but rest on 1–2 usable arms, because the test set was drawn around the tree's skills. Item by item, the tree adds a little over these baselines (CV R² +0.006) and they add a little over the tree (+0.002). A fair scheme-vs-scheme test needs test questions drawn for the baseline groups too.

Checks on alternative explanations

Memorised answer wordings

Some benchmarks reuse fixed option wordings (e.g. DSI-Bench's camera options recur dozens of times). A model could gain by learning which wording tends to be right, without looking at the video, and siblings drawn from the same benchmark would share those wordings. To test this, we ran a text-only phrasing learner through the same analysis code: for each arm it scores each test option by how often that exact wording was the correct answer in the arm's training questions, and nothing else.

C3 own vs sibling+2.71+2.17+2.78

Exact-wording priors can account for part of the within-group effect (C1, C3) but not for sibling-over-far transfer, which is negative for this learner. A model could still generalise from similar wordings, which this check does not capture. On the Camera Motion test questions specifically, the same rule scores 44 % against 29 % chance (79 % on DSI-Bench), so the within-group Camera Motion gain is likely inflated by answer priors.

Frozen vision tower

All runs above trained LoRA on the language model only; the vision encoder was frozen. That could push training toward text-side cues and explain why gains also appear blind. We are re-running the tree core (7 whole + 16 leave-one-out + 3 random arms × 3 seeds) with LoRA also on every vision-encoder block and the merger (110 extra modules, 8.0 M extra parameters), paired run for run with the frozen runs. The full analysis was written before any result.

So far, adapting the vision tower changes overall accuracy by a fraction of a point and does not lower blind gains. The own-group change with video varies widely across runs. No conclusion until the random and leave-one-out arms finish.

Limits

  • One model. The plan pools C2 over two models; Gemma-4-12B is not trained yet (it often answers with a word instead of a letter, still unresolved).
  • Gains are small and partly text-side. Random training alone raises p(correct) by +4.3 pp with video; tree effects are a few points on top, and the video-specific part of C2 is not significant (p 0.058).
  • Dominated by one group. Without Camera Motion, sibling transfer is about +1.7 pp with video.
  • Same-benchmark training. Train and test questions come from the same benchmarks. Next step: train on public video training sets outside these benchmarks (candidates identified for Camera Motion, after removing clips that share source videos with DSI-Bench), and check that the phrasing learner scores at chance on them first.
  • Labels are model-made. Skill labels come from an LLM labeller. Random label noise would weaken tree effects rather than create them, but systematic labeller habits (e.g. labelling by benchmark style) could mimic them.

Runs and compute

Gate runs (18), tree grid (87), semantic mirror (72 + cross-scoring), unfrozen-vision runs so far (20): about 136 A100-hours on Harvard's Cannon cluster. No paid API calls in training or scoring.

Every training run is scored on all 2,129 test questions × {video, blind} (4,258 rows); no rows missing. Model: Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B at a pinned revision, thinking off, answer letter read at a fixed position.
